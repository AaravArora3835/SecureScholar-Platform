## Module Overview

**Topic:** Phishing Defense  
**Target Audience:** High school students (ages 14-18)  
**Learning Goal:** Students will identify phishing attempts and know how to respond safely

**Why This Module Matters:**
Phishing is the #1 way hackers gain access to accounts and personal information. High school students are active online but often lack training to spot sophisticated scams. This module gives them practical, memorable skills to protect themselves and their families.

---

## Module Structure

- **4 lessons** (200-300 words each)
- **10 quiz questions** (4 beginner, 4 intermediate, 2 advanced)
- **"Aarav's Note"** in each lesson (50-100 words - personal insight/story)
- **Interactive examples** (screenshots of fake vs real emails)

## Lesson Breakdown

### Lesson 1: What is Phishing?
**Key Points to Cover:**
- Definition: fraudulent attempt to obtain sensitive info by pretending to be trustworthy
- Real-world impact (Verizon DBIR: 36% of breaches involve phishing)
- Basic example: fake Netflix password reset email
- Psychology: why smart people fall for it (urgency, authority, fear)
- Your digital identity has real-world consequences

**Target Word Count:** 250 words  
**Status:** ✅ Draft Complete (Oct 31)

**Aarav's Note Theme:** Personal story about almost clicking a phishing link or seeing family member fall for one

---

### Lesson 2: Types of Phishing
**Key Points to Cover:**
- Email phishing (mass campaigns)
- Spear phishing (targeted, personalized attacks)
- Smishing (SMS/text message phishing)
- Vishing (voice/phone call phishing)
- Real examples of each type students might encounter

**Target Word Count:** 250 words  
**Status:** ⚪ Not Started

**Aarav's Note Theme:** Which type is hardest to spot and why

---

### Lesson 3: How to Spot Phishing
**Key Points to Cover:**
- Check sender email address (hover, don't click)
- Look for urgency or threats ("account will be closed!")
- Grammar and spelling errors
- Requests for personal information
- Too-good-to-be-true offers
- Suspicious links (hover to preview URL)

**Target Word Count:** 250 words  
**Status:** ⚪ Not Started

**Aarav's Note Theme:** The one trick that never fails to spot fakes

---

### Lesson 4: What to Do If You're Targeted
**Key Points to Cover:**
- DON'T click links or download attachments
- DON'T reply (even to say "stop")
- DO report to IT/teacher/parent immediately
- DO change passwords if you clicked
- DO enable 2FA on important accounts
- Learn from the attempt - what fooled you?

**Target Word Count:** 250 words  
**Status:** ⚪ Not Started

**Aarav's Note Theme:** What to tell friends/family who fell for phishing (no judgment, just action)
